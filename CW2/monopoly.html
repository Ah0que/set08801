Introduction to Monopoly


Monopoly is a classic board game where players move around the game board, buying, trading, or renting properties with the goal of becoming the wealthiest player through monopolizing the economy. Players collect rent from opponents landing on their properties, with the ultimate goal of driving opponents into bankruptcy.

Components of the Game


A standard Monopoly game includes:

Game board with properties (Streets, Railroads, Utilities), Chance, Community Chest, and other spaces.
Dice (two standard six-sided dice).
Tokens (player pieces).
Title Deed cards for each property.
Chance cards.
Community Chest cards.
Money (various denominations).
Houses and Hotels (plastic or wooden buildings).

Setting Up the Game


Follow these steps to set up:

Place the board on a flat surface.
Place the Chance and Community Chest cards face down on their respective spaces on the board.
Each player chooses a token and places it on the "GO" space.
The Banker is chosen (one player who manages the bank's money, properties not yet bought, houses, and hotels). The Banker can also play, but must keep their personal money separate.
The Banker gives each player their starting money. The standard amount varies by version, but a common distribution is:
Two $500s
Two $100s
Two $50s
Six $20s
Five $10s
Five $5s
Five $1s
Players roll the dice; the player with the highest roll goes first. Play proceeds to the left.

Gameplay - Taking a Turn


On your turn, you:

Roll both dice and move your token clockwise around the board the number of spaces indicated by the sum of the dice.
Perform an action based on the space you land on.
If you rolled doubles, you take your turn, and then roll the dice again for another turn. Be careful: rolling doubles three times in a row sends you to Jail.
Your turn ends after you complete your action (unless you rolled doubles).

Landing on Spaces


What happens when you land on different spaces:

Unowned Property (Street, Railroad, Utility): You have the option to buy the property for the price listed on the board.
If you buy it, pay the Bank the price and take the corresponding Title Deed card.
If you do not want to buy it, the property is immediately put up for auction by the Banker. Any player, including the one who declined to buy, can bid. Bidding starts at $1, and the property goes to the highest bidder.
Owned Property: If another player owns the property, you must pay them rent. The amount is listed on the Title Deed card and depends on whether they own a Monopoly (all properties of that color group) and if there are houses or hotels built.
If the property is mortgaged, you do not pay rent.
The owner must ask for rent before the next player rolls the dice, or they forfeit the rent.
Chance or Community Chest: Draw the top card from the respective deck and follow the instructions on the card. Return the card to the bottom of the deck after use (unless it's a "Get Out of Jail Free" card).
Tax Spaces (Income Tax, Luxury Tax): Pay the amount indicated to the Bank.
"GO": When you land on or pass "GO" during your turn, you collect $200 from the Bank.
Just Visiting (Jail): If you land on this space, you are simply visiting Jail and are not incarcerated. Place your token in the "Just Visiting" section.
Go to Jail: Move your token directly to the "In Jail" space. Your turn ends immediately. You do not collect $200 for passing GO.
Free Parking: Landing on this space has no effect in standard rules, though many house rules add a bonus here.

Jail


You can end up in Jail by:

Landing on the "Go to Jail" space.
Drawing a Chance or Community Chest card that says "Go to Jail".
Rolling doubles three consecutive times on your turn.
While in Jail:

You cannot move your token around the board.
You can still buy houses and hotels, collect rent, and participate in auctions if you are not bankrupt.
To get out of Jail:

Pay a $50 fine before you roll the dice on either of your next two turns.
Use a "Get Out of Jail Free" card before you roll the dice on any subsequent turn.
Attempt to roll doubles on your next three turns.
If you succeed on your first or second attempt, you get out of Jail and move your token the number of spaces shown by the doubles roll.
If you fail to roll doubles on your third attempt, you must pay the $50 fine and then move your token the number of spaces shown by the roll.
Once you are out of Jail, you move your token and continue your turn as normal.

Buying and Building


Property ownership is key to winning:

Monopolies: When you own all properties of the same color group, you have a Monopoly. You can then build houses and hotels on any property within that group. You also collect double rent on any unimproved property in that group.
Houses: You can buy houses from the Bank during your turn, after you have landed on a space and completed any action. You must build evenly, meaning you cannot build a second house on a property until you have one house on all properties in that color group. The maximum number of houses per property is four.
Hotels: Once you have four houses on all properties in a color group, you can buy a hotel. A hotel replaces the four houses on that property. You can only have one hotel per property.
Selling Buildings: Houses and hotels can be sold back to the Bank for half the price you paid for them. You must sell evenly, meaning you cannot sell a house from one property if it leaves that property with more houses than another property in the same color group.
Building Limits: There are a limited number of houses and hotels in the game. If the Bank runs out, you cannot buy more until some are returned.

Mortgaging Properties


If you need money, you can mortgage your properties:

Only properties without any buildings on them can be mortgaged. If you want to mortgage a property with buildings, you must sell all buildings in that color group back to the Bank first.
Flip the Title Deed card over to the "Mortgaged" side. The Bank pays you the mortgage value listed on the card.
You do not collect rent on a mortgaged property.
To unmortgage a property, pay the Bank the mortgage value plus 10% interest. Flip the Title Deed card back to the "Unmortgaged" side.

Bankruptcy


If you owe money to the Bank or another player and cannot pay, you are bankrupt.

If you owe the Bank and cannot pay, the Bank takes all your assets (money, properties, Get Out of Jail Free cards). Any mortgaged properties are unmortgaged by the Bank (the 10% interest is not paid), and then immediately put up for auction.
If you owe another player and cannot pay, that player receives all your assets. Any mortgaged properties are given to that player, who must immediately either unmortgage them (paying the Bank the mortgage value plus 10% interest) or keep them mortgaged (but must still pay the 10% interest to the Bank immediately).
Once bankrupt, you are out of the game.

Winning the Game


The game continues until only one player remains who is not bankrupt. That player is the winner!

Conclusion


Monopoly is a game of chance, strategy, and negotiation. Understanding the rules for moving, buying, building, and managing your money are key to success. Gather your friends or family and enjoy the game!
