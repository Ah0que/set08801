Basic Guide on How to Play Tag Rugby

Introduction to Tag Rugby


Tag Rugby is a non-contact version of rugby union or league. Instead of tackling, players remove a tag from the ball carrier's belt to stop their progress. It's a fast-paced and inclusive sport suitable for all ages and skill levels.

Objective of the Game


The main objective is to score tries by carrying the ball across the opponent's try line and grounding it. The team with the most tries at the end of the game wins.

Players and Equipment


Two teams, typically with 7 players per side on the field at a time (though this can vary).
A rugby ball (usually size 4 or 5 depending on age).
Tag belts with two tags attached, worn by each player.
Cones or markers to mark the field boundaries and try lines.

Setting Up the Field


Tag Rugby is played on a rectangular field, usually grass, similar in shape to a rugby or football pitch but often smaller. Mark out the boundaries and try lines at each end using cones.

Basic Gameplay

Starting the Game


The game usually starts with a kick-off or a tap-and-pass from the center of the field.

Moving the Ball


Players can run with the ball.
The ball can only be passed backwards or sideways to a teammate. Forward passes are not allowed.
Players cannot intentionally knock the ball forward (a "knock-on").

Tagging


Instead of tackling, defenders stop the ball carrier by pulling one of their tags off their belt.
When a tag is removed, the ball carrier must stop running and pass the ball immediately (usually within 3 seconds).
The defender who removed the tag holds it up and usually returns it to the attacking player before rejoining the game.
After a tag, the attacking team restarts play from where the tag was made with a tap-and-pass.

Six Tags


A team has a limited number of tags (usually six) before possession of the ball is turned over to the other team. After the sixth tag, the referee will call a turnover.

Scoring a Try


A try is scored when a player carries the ball across the opponent's try line and grounds it (presses it down on the ground).
A try is typically worth 1 point in many forms of Tag Rugby.

Defence


Defenders try to prevent the attacking team from scoring by:

Removing the ball carrier's tags.
Staying in a defensive line between the attackers and their try line.
Trying to intercept passes.

Restarting Play


After a try is scored, the scoring team usually kicks off to the other team from the center.
If the ball goes out of bounds, play restarts with a tap-and-pass from the sideline.
Penalties can be awarded for various infringements (like forward passes, offside, or not passing after being tagged), resulting in a tap-and-pass for the non-offending team.

Basic Rules Summary


Non-contact sport.
Remove a tag instead of tackling.
Pass backwards or sideways.
Score tries by grounding the ball over the try line.
Usually a limit of six tags per possession.

Getting Started


The best way to learn Tag Rugby is to join a local club or group that plays. They will teach you the specific rules and variations they use. It's a fun and social way to get active!

Conclusion


Tag Rugby is an accessible and enjoyable sport that keeps the spirit of rugby without the physical contact. Understanding how to move the ball, tag opponents, and score tries are the key basics to get you started. Have fun playing!
